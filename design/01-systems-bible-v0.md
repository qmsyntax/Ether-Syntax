## LAW 0 — The shape of the system
- [F] This is **not a stat-sheet LitRPG**. No STR/DEX/INT, no classes, no levels. The numbers that exist:
  star count, % progress within a star ("essence capacity"), ability **stage** + % progress,
  healing **grade**, item % modifiers, and dollars. → *Game implication: a lean, legible HUD; depth
  comes from interactions, not stat inflation.*
- [C] Money is always concrete. Prices are part of the fiction's voice.

## LAW 1 — Ether
- [C] Ether is ambient energy that **becomes like what it touches** (ether shift). Shifted motes are **wisps**; collecting them is **gathering**.
- [F] **Essence** is the arch's spendable pool (abilities and healing are priced in essence). Harvested from kills and in the Netherecho.
- [F][?] "Lifespark" = a creature's life-force, normally only manipulable around 4 stars.
- [F] Local ether mirrors the place: water at sea, fire at a volcano. → *Biome decides what you can gather.*

## LAW 2 — Souls, stars, ascension
- [C] The soul's inner space is the **ethercosm**; rank = **stars** in it. A new star is blank white until attuned by its first affinity wisp.
- [F] Stars run 1–6. 4★ ≈ city lord / clan head. 5★ = the Empress. No human 6★. Progress within a star is a %, with a wall at 99%.
- [C] 1★ is near-mortal with no presence. From 2★: body changes + **aura** that sickens lower ranks unless suppressed. [F] Netherecho perception ≈ +10 ft per star.
- [F] Advancement currency is **latent aura**, earned only through *genuine* difficulty (dangerous kills, building quality abilities, crafting, mental trials). You may ascend at minimum or over-fill for a stronger ascension.
- [F] **Growth limiters** (reduce aura gain): fighting in groups, shortcut consumables, too many abilities (~22 at 2★ is the tipping point), many affinities, just following orders, complacency.
  → *This is a gift for a crawler: the universe itself justifies solo play, anti-grind scaling, and "no free lunch" consumables.*
- [F] Ascending may grant a new, usually related, affinity. [F] Hybrid and unique affinities exist (e.g. "life flame", "Perished Water").
- [F][?] Characters have a **"satellite"** with its own star rank — meaning unknown.

## LAW 3 — Talents and Prime Vestiges
- [C] One talent per person, **permanent**, delivered by a **prime vestige** (sentient talking orb) that also carries 1+ affinities. Primes answer truthfully what they know, often cryptically.
- [C][F] Sources of primes: a mortal surviving/killing monsters (chance, not certainty); [F] an arch killing a **deviant** monster.
- [C] Value: combat > non-combat; fewer restrictions > more; more affinities ≫ fewer. Cheap prime ≈ six figures; top lots tens of millions. Appraisal is slow and costs as much as a cheap prime.
- [C] Talents **evolve with rank**.
- [C] A talent's fine print is discovered by experiment.

### The title talent
- [C][F] Deal harm to a living thing → recover 1% of it. "Enemy" turns out to be **anything alive** — grass, trees, food. [F]
- [F] Healing grades: Minimal → First-Aid → Natural → Supernatural → Supreme (essence cost climbs steeply). Lifesteal heals at the top grade (regrows limbs, fixes congenital defects, reverses ageing) [?] wiki's Book 5 sheet calls it "Dynamic" — naming conflict.
- [F] **Too slow to matter mid-fight.** Its power is *between* fights: it makes self-destructive training, body-priced abilities and reckless all-offence sustainable.
  → *Design north star: Lifesteal is not a combat sustain stat. It is a licence to pay costs nobody else can afford.*

## LAW 4 — Abilities
- [C] Learned from **ether-imprint scrolls** (read once, written to the soul), gated by affinity. Can also be **self-authored** after understanding a principle.
- [C][F] Types: **tempering techniques** (passive; work by damaging the body so it adapts), **active abilities** (grouped by affinity: strikes, mobility, spells, constructs), **spirit abilities**.
- [C] Using a technique your body isn't tempered for injures you.
- [F] **Stages**: an ability is upgraded by binding an ether construct into it — vestige 0→1, remnant 1→2, spirit 2→3, eidolon 3→4, (asura 4→5). The construct's **concept** (Acceleration, Piercing, Compression…) shapes the upgrade. → *This is the socket/gem system, already designed for us.*
- [F] **Spirit abilities**: must be **spoken aloud**; always carry a **price and/or trigger condition** (progress, limbs, life-force, life); each slows soul growth. In-world belief: three are needed to pass 5★.
- [F] **Soul construct**: a personal Netherecho tool (protagonist: a scythe that speeds essence collection and can absorb items).
- [F] **Cursed growth items**: uniques with % bonuses and a growth condition (spill blood; beat stronger foes); absorbable into the soul construct.

## LAW 5 — The Netherecho and ether constructs
- [C] A concept-layer of reality entered by projecting the soul. Things appear as painted caricatures; wisps drift as paint. **Projection dies → you die.** [F] Damage there is soul damage. [F] Only stationary/repetitive real-world things show up.
- [C][F] Inhabitants = **ether constructs**, embodiments of concepts, which **cannot lie**: wisp → **vestige** → **remnant** → **spirit** → **eidolon** → **asura**. They grow by absorbing ether or eating each other. Spirits can briefly manifest; eidolons exist in both planes.
- [F] Rough threat parity: vestige ≤ peak 1★; remnant 2★–early 3★; spirit ≤ early 4★; eidolon peak 4★+; asura 5–6★.
- [C] Blood-soaked objects from passage slaughter become **catalysts** tied to rare constructs; constructs can ride a vessel out.

## LAW 6 — Passages, realms, breaks, delving
- [C] **Passages** lead to **realms** (catalogue codes; can dwarf Earth; many entrances). Passages have a **step count**; zero-step = direct link to New Earth = geopolitical event.
- [C][F] **Breaks**: realm inhabitants spill into the city. Responders clear them; mortals get no bounty.
- [F] **Hubs** are commercial facilities on passages (one example: free entry, takes **half** of what you carry out).
- [F] **Delvers** work in parties and haul **whole corpses** out — carry capacity is an economic stat. (Tension with LAW 2: parties earn more money but less latent aura.)
- [F] **Deviants**: off-pattern monsters; more dangerous; the arch route to a prime drop.
- [F] Realm ecologies have their own clocks (e.g. a hive realm with a periodic red-sky frenzy).
- [F] **Trial dungeon**: single-entrant special passage with extreme time dilation, run by a spirit of Insanity, rewards for surviving.
- [F] Debtors can be indentured to mine clan-owned realms.

## LAW 7 — Society
- [F] **The American Empire**: might-makes-right under a 5★ Empress; city lords near-absolute locally, checked by pragmatism and imperial adjudicators. [C] Lords and clan heads deal via binding **contracts**.
- [F] **Banks are untouchable** even by the strong (hard-learned rule). [C] Taxes, insurance, bureaucracy all function.
- [C] **ATA** brokers primes and gear; tiered service; lethal security.
- [F] **Northern Belt**: lawless frozen ex-Canada, wild low-tier passages and realm fragments, easy in / hard out.
- [F] Factions: Kraven clan (blood affinity, mines), Gem family (loan sharks), Valhalla (Belt vigilantes), a first-generation villain's cult, Madame Morleppe (4★ patron/media), "square-eyed" possessors spreading an "ether virus", mainland clans' "young masters".
- [F] All ascended beings understand each other's speech; mortals don't.
- [F] Death-affinity **undeath** is a real state with real trade-offs; the protagonist enters it.

# Additions from Book 5 (ch 203–225, read directly — all [C]; details in research/book5-mechanics-notes.md)

## LAW 2b — Rank detail
- Within a star: **low / mid / high / peak**, plus a separate **standard vs elite** quality label. An elite 2★ can beat a standard 3★.
- Ascension = the current star "giving birth" to the next. Readiness **cannot be tested externally**.
- **How you climbed matters**: safe climbers (elixirs, aids, minimum requirements) end up ordinary with weak presence; risk-takers become Lords. Most repeat gamblers die.
- Stars can be **lost**. You can re-climb only along your old path (faster), unless a **severing elixir** wipes it for a clean rebuild.
- **Triple Block**: three spirit abilities ⇒ ascension cost ×100. Publicly, nobody has broken it. (Reconciles the fan claim that three are "needed" past 5★ [?].)
- Ability **stage cap appears to be star count + 1** [I]. New hybrid affinities start at Stage 0.
- Abilities gain **named upgrades** as they advance (e.g. "tension": charge a throw indefinitely, even until your arm breaks; "anti-gravity"; "acceleration").

## LAW 1b — Essence economy in a fight
- **Essence** = % pool stored in the stars; ability costs are quoted in %.
- **Reflux essence** = residue left in the body by every ability use, tracked **per affinity** as a %. Dissipates slowly; a tempering technique speeds it. It is the real limiter on sustained casting. → *A per-element heat gauge.*
- **Raw essence manipulation** (shape your element without an ability): always available, weak, safe.
- Regions have **ether density** (ether deserts vs ether-rich frontier): richer = deadlier and more lucrative.
- Efficient abilities look plain; flash is wasted essence.
- **There is no system UI.** The protagonist keeps a handwritten sheet: Gathering / Talent / True Soul Construct / Tempering Techniques / Active Abilities / Cursed Items. → *Diegetic notebook HUD.*

## LAW 3b — The talent, late-game truth
- Sheet line: "Dynamic-quality healing". "Supreme-quality" is a separate, also-named grade — so the ladder has at least two top labels [?] order unknown.
- Heals any bodily injury; effectively biological immortality; body is rebuilt toward the **soul's template** of it. Brain destruction is the implied kill condition.
- **Cannot** heal the soul or shifts in brain chemistry. He can be drained, starved of physique, or eaten while out.
- A summon's kills heal him too.
- Canon exploits: limbs as ammunition; hit-trading; drug side-effects tanked for permanent gains; regrown-organ sales (until a conglomerate cloned them and crashed the market); 7 dungeons a day vs a norm of one a week.
- Double-talented people exist; talents can be upgraded later; people routinely under-report what their talent does.

## LAW 4b — Soul constructs, spirit abilities, items
- A **soul construct** can be **awakened** into a "true" one with a **prime remnant**, found only in an affinity's **primordial domain** after a chain of trials. Almost nobody knows this.
- His awakened construct is a **weapon modifier**: wraps anything he can demonstrably wield; a "soul muscle" carries the weight (overload ⇒ forced retract, ~5 min); per kill **+1% essence and ≈−1% reflux on every affinity**; absorbs **one regular + one unique item**, mutating their effects toward weapon-modding; **only explains an effect after he discovers it himself**; it talks.
- **Spirit abilities** are tradeable acquisitions; cost-free ones sell for fortunes. Example shape: ~10 s of being slowed, then ~5 s at double subjective speed — so players pre-cast during a countdown.
- A **bonded spirit summon**: size scales with blood fed to it; fights like a competent caster a star above; its kills count as the summoner's.
- **Items ("cursed items"/artifacts)**: typically ONE effect ≤10%. **Sets** = three items fused with a **Gem of Binding** (natural = dungeon reward; "Superior" binds four; factory "Inferior" ≈ $6.2B, ≤90% success, no uniques, needs a master artificer, "sister concepts" only).
- **Unique items grow** when world events match their growth condition — with diminishing returns and plateaus. Items have **concepts**.
- Catalogue tiers: B $1–5B, A $5–10B, S $10B+ (auction only).

## LAW 6b — Interspace and dungeons (the crawler canon)
- Passages open into the **interspace**: a non-Euclidean web of realms; depth in **steps**; realms have **letter classes** (B, C seen). Useful chains become fee-charging **portal hubs**. One passage leads to another planet.
- **Wild interspace** (open, lethal, best money near high-ether zones) vs **dungeons**.
- **Dungeons = "anomalous realms"**: they conjure false people and monsters from nothing; *nothing but the reward is real*. Named types:
  - **Resetting** (restored to an identical state); **resource caches** let a resource type be carried out every reset.
  - **Instance** dungeons, incl. unexplored "mystery" instances with suicidal scouting bounties.
  - **Scenario** dungeons: fortress defence, mountain-pass assault, chambers-then-boss (rated for 20), trap mazes needing a scout.
  - **Trial/prison**: a century of subjective solitude with a madness entity and a three-way reward choice.
  - A **tower** whose entry slots are auctioned; earlier entrants do better.
- Observed rules: **party caps** (5, 20); **entrance seals** at a marker; **minimum progress to unlock the exit** (5 of 10 walls); escalating stages of troops → elites → commander; difficulty spike past midpoint; enemies adapt; environmental hazards.
- **Rewards are per person at the exit, scaled to personal contribution**; repeats pay less; some are once-per-person; extraordinary feats can yield a unique and permanently close the dungeon.
- Rated by rank + party size ("advanced two-star party").
- **Access economy**: good passages are faction-owned; entry **tickets** are auctioned; auction houses apply **bidding bonuses/penalties by faction standing** (+300% for allies, −50% from a hostile house). Tickets are also wagered.
- Norms: >1 delve/week is "too much"; mid-2★ dungeon ≈ $3–6M/clear; party splits are negotiated %.

## LAW 7b — Reputation and the state
- **Badges/medals**: bronze → silver → gold → platinum → violet → white "special". War tracks (kill counts, above-rank kills, giant one-shots, time in combat, battles survived) and civil tracks (Dungeoneer / **Solo Dungeoneer** by full clears, wealth, arts…). Essence-signed, **audited** (implausible records get you detained and re-tested), gate vendors and services, and are the **currency of political favours**. Deliberately no badge for delving *speed*.
- **Lordship Qualification Standard**: personal power is essentially the only criterion for holding land. Adjudicators (4★) deed and confiscate.
- The empire frames everything as the **Eternal War**; monster-slaying is military service.
- **Arena**: simulated, fully painful, consequence-free, **grants no latent aura**. Modes: ranked, performance, honour duels, waves, mock raids. Its meta is degenerate precisely because nothing is at stake.

## LAW 9 — Soul damage (long-term injury system)
- The soul is the seat of choice and experience. Damage ⇒ emotional flatness, lost time, pain on ability use, **ascension blocked**; spirit abilities and raw manipulation still work.
- Heals only through **significant lived experience** (grief counts more than fun); sleep does nothing. **Soul elixirs** patch with blank tissue — fast, but for severe damage you gradually become someone else.
- How it works is a **legal infohazard**.

## Rulings 2026-09-23 (Brent, after playing v0.6.0)
- [B] **Base melee is single-target and Diablo-style**: click an enemy to walk to it and hit *that* enemy (hold to keep hitting); click ground to walk there. Area hits belong to skills (Tsunami Strike), never to the base attack.
- [B] **Stages are gated by constructs.** An ability's bar fills to 100 and then waits: stage 0 full needs a **vestige**, stage 1 full a **remnant**, and so on up the tiers. Each construct's **concept** shapes the upgrade.
- [B] **You start with a baby star.** Each star's essence capacity grows toward 99%; a new star after ascension starts at **1%**. [MODEL] the tank is full_tank × capacity; capacity grows from kills (by the enemy's aura value) and from meditation; ascension needs the newest star at 99% *and* the hidden latent aura.

## LAW 4c — Runes (Book 5 ch 230, read directly 2026-09-23; Brent: "like the incantations that define a skill")
- [C] An ability is a spherical **ether cage** holding its concepts. Unravelled onto a flat surface it is a structure of **runes**; there is a known rune and **microrune alphabet** with grammar, and essence-imprint forms of each.
- [C] **Runechains** are linear strings of concepts progressing from less to more sophisticated — the part of an ability that *levels*. When a chain "suffulates", spacer microconcepts appear at prime positions and the chain must grow exponentially to improve further. The first ten spacers have short names (em, in, sum, ol, lun, slo, efi, ofo, gi, al). Concepts gated behind manipulation talents live almost entirely in these chains.
- [C] Disciplines: **runesetting** (building abilities, enchanting, making cursed items), **runeology** (the study; taught per affinity and in basic/advanced levels), **auragraphy** (writing runes), **enchanting** (runes written onto items), **abilitycrafting**. A full "runepedia" costs millions; a self-taught fighter has the instinct but not the theory.
- [C] The empress fears an uneducated fighter making an **irreversible mistake in his development** (ch 227) — ability construction has permanent consequences.
- → *Game reading*: the ethercosm screen shows each ability as a ring of runes around the star; the runechain is its progress bar with visible spacer gaps past each stage; runesetting is the eventual ability-crafting system. Details and open items in `research/book5-ch226-230-notes.md`.
- [C, ch 226–229] Also new: storage rings are used by sinking attention into them, have an internal layout (items can be stowed "deep" to avoid mis-pulls) and fully mask an item's aura; **two tiers of ascension elixir** — ordinary aids inject blank aura and can degrade a talent with overuse, a true elixir also amplifies the evolution; more badge tracks (Guardian, Entrepreneur, Adventurer). Body durability and especially the nervous system scale with rank.

## LAW 10 — Deep cosmology
- Each affinity has a **primordial domain** (water's: an endless storm ocean under a leviathan-scale watcher); time runs long inside; trials happen there; prime remnants come from there.
- A person can birth a **fate** through extreme drive.
- In the Netherecho, conceptually "right" but impractical weapons work better than in reality.

## What this means for a dungeon crawler (first pass)
1. **Solo-first by canon.** Parties = more loot, less growth. Make that an explicit per-delve choice.
2. **Costs, not cooldowns.** Strong abilities charge HP, limbs, growth, or future options. The title talent is what lets the player afford them — *between* rooms/fights, by harming living things (yes, including the scenery).
3. **Sockets are creatures.** Upgrading an ability means hunting/bargaining for a construct with the right *concept* in the Netherecho layer.
4. **Two currencies that fight each other:** dollars (parties, hubs, hauling) vs latent aura (solo, hardship, no shortcuts).
5. **Anti-bloat is canon, but soft:** there is no cap. Each ability competes for the same essence and the same play time to level, so builds stay tight because a wide build ascends with half-finished abilities `[B]`.
6. **Break clock:** the overworld punishes ignoring passages.
7. **Dungeons are already game-shaped in canon.** Anomalous realms reset, seal behind you, demand minimum progress to open the exit, scale loot to personal contribution, pay less on repeats, and sometimes close forever after a feat. We don't have to invent crawler rules — we have to implement his.
8. **Heat, not mana.** Per-affinity reflux is the pacing system; kills purge it (with the right construct). Aggression is the cooldown reset.
9. **The HUD is a notebook** the character writes in, and the weapon companion only confirms effects you've discovered. Discovery is diegetic.
10. **Meta-progression = badges + faction standing**, which gate vendors and skew ticket auctions. Solo clears have their own badge track — canon rewards the solo crawler.
11. **Risk is the only XP.** The arena (safe) gives nothing. Safe climbing produces a visibly worse character. This is the design's spine.
12. **Permanent loss exists**: gear destroyed, stars lost, soul damage that only life events heal. Roguelite-with-scars rather than clean permadeath.

# Rulings from Brent (audiobooks 1–4, from memory, 2026-09-18) — tag [B]
These override fan sources where they conflict.

**Progression**
- [B] **Latent aura is hidden XP.** Nobody sees a number. At threshold it lets the **talent evolve** and the soul **split off another star**.
- [B] **Spirit abilities compress latent aura**: each one delays the next star, but makes each ascension a **bigger jump in talent power**. (So Triple Block ×100 is the extreme end of a deliberate trade, not just a penalty.)
- [B] Compression is **33% per spirit ability: 33% / 66% / 99%**. At three, only 1% of aura counts ⇒ the ×100 Triple Block from Book 5 is this same rule at its limit.
- [B] **Reflux essence** is shed by the body naturally and **purged faster by meditation**. It is a genuine limiting mechanic.
- [B] **Stars are independent essence tanks.** More stars = more tanks.
- [B, 2026-09-23] **Satellites are not free and do not come with ascension.** They are things you **find or buy** — items, in game terms. A fresh archhuman has none. (The prototype's built-in 1/s satellite is a placeholder until inventory exists.) Brent also describes the ethercosm view as showing the stars with the character's skills and **runes** around them — a term not yet in these notes; define it with him when the Ethercosm screen is designed.
- [B] **Satellite** = passive essence throughput. A 2★ satellite supplies **2% essence per second**; an ability that costs ≤2%/s drains nothing from the tanks. Satellite flow **also produces reflux**. (Reflux itself is still not fully understood — see spec for our working model.)
- [B] The "too many abilities" growth penalty is **not a rule, it is an opportunity cost** (clarified 2026-09-23; supersedes the wiki's limiter list and my earlier "remove from design" note). Abilities are skilled up by **spending essence through them and using them**. Take on too many and you cannot get them all up before ascension — too many plates to spin. By Book 4 he has ~16 *completed* abilities, which is the point: he had the essence supply and the delving hours to finish them.

**The talent**
- [B] Evolution confirmed: **1★ = Supreme-quality healing only → 2★ = Dynamic-quality.** Meaning: healing arrives as a budget. Supreme spends ~100 points per unit of tissue no matter what; lower grades (first-aid/natural) spend ~1. Dynamic lets the budget be spent at whatever grade each wound actually needs, so the same income heals far more of the body.
- [B] No canon numbers. Bigger monsters carry more vitality to steal from. **For the game: plain damage and HP.**
- [B] **Overkill does not count** (capped at the target's remaining HP). **DoTs he caused do pay out.**
- [B] Does **not** fix: soul exhaustion, need for sleep, essence depletion. Keeps the brain physically healthy but **psychological trauma persists**.

**Abilities, constructs, Netherecho**
- [B] Binding a construct: the construct is **subdued**; it is **irreversible**; it **can fail**; the construct/spirit/eidolon **can kill you**.
- [B] **All tempering techniques run at once**; they simply make the body tougher.
- [B] The Netherecho has **no tiers of its own** — it's just a place where you run into tiered things (wisps, vestiges, remnants, spirits, eidola).
- [B] The **soul construct is granted at first star**. Upgrade path unknown; in Book 4 he obtained the thing (named in Book 5 as the prime remnant) that awakened it.
- [B] Constructs **don't want anything** — they are concepts that simply exist. (So: no bargaining economy; they are subdued, fled from, or avoided.)
- Unknown to Brent: how self-authored spells work; whether scrolls are consumed / abilities taught.

**Passages, dungeons, monsters**
- [B] Difficulty is expressed as a **recommended rank/party** ("good for a high 2★", "a group of 1★s"), not a formal grade.
- [B] Realms contain **both** open wilderness and bounded dungeon structures.
- [B] Why passages open / break is **not yet explained** in the books. They just open.
- [B] Hub access rules are **whatever the owner says**. Inside, **the universe sets each dungeon's or space's rules at its creation.**
- [B] Monsters have **stars, affinities and talents**. Book 4: all monsters have **native stars that are not "ascended"**.
- [B] The Century of Solitude has **no rules — just time.**

**Gear, drugs**
- [B] (2026-10-11) **Affinities decide which skills you can learn and are often unrelated to the prime's talent.** At character creation **three primes are rolled** from the table of prime abilities; each rolls a number of **affinity slots - 1 common, 2 rare, 3 very very rare (max 3)** - and each slot is filled at random. [MODEL] the exact odds (87.5 / 12 / 0.5 %), equal chance per affinity, and a limited reroll for testing.
- [B] (2026-10-11) **Abilities do not harm their caster by default.** Tsunami Strike's self-harm in the books came from one specific combination of concepts and skills the protagonist assembled; it is not a property of the strike.
- [B] (2026-10-11) **Health is one bar plus wounds.** The three wound layers never made a difference in play. Each wound has an invisible pool of healing that must be filled before it closes, separate from and not shown in the health bar. **Talent healing:** if it is not Dynamic it goes to the wound pools first; if it is Dynamic it fills the health bar to 50%, then the pools. [MODEL] what causes a wound, its size, the cut to maximum health, and what rest and the healer do.
- [B] (2026-10-11) **The second star should take about two hours of play**, not ten minutes. Skill progress should slow too, later - it is left fast while testing.
- [B] Gear matters but **less than the body**. It is crafted, quested for, awarded, and dropped by monsters.
- [B] Drugs have ordinary downsides; archhumans are so healthy they can abuse far more than mortals.
