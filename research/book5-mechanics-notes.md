## 1. Power system

### Ranks ("gathering" / stars)
- [S] Power rank = number of **stars** held in the soul; the process is called **gathering**; going up is **ascending**, described as the current star giving birth to the next (ch204). Protagonist's sheet reads second star, ready to ascend, with a "2-star satellite" line (ch204) - meaning of "satellite" undefined here.
- [S] Observed ladder: mortal (no star) -> one-star -> two-star -> three-star -> four-star -> five-star. Five-star = **Overlord** (ch205). [I] four-star = the tier of **Lords/Ladies**, adjudicators, judges, investigators (ch206, 208, 213: a retired Lord "could have become an Overlord"). The empress's essence signature reads as five-star (ch206); a former adjudicator privately believes the protagonist's ceiling is above hers (ch218).
- [S] Within a star people are graded low-level / mid-level / high-level / peak, and separately "standard" vs "elite" for the same rank (ch224-225). A two-star elite may beat a standard three-star.
- [S] **Latent aura** is the fuel for ascension. It accrues only from real risk - simulated arena fights give none (ch214); heavy delving is a known way to pile it up (ch223).
- [S] There is no known external test for whether someone's star is ready to ascend (ch209).
- [S] **Quality of the climb matters**: four-stars who advanced the safe way (elixirs, gathering aids, time, minimum requirements) look ordinary, lack presence and get backwater postings; risk-takers become Lords (ch206). A cautious veteran reflects that repeated gamblers usually die, except for rare people who seem immune to the odds (ch218, 223).
- [S] **Losing stars** is possible (the adjudicator fell from four to two defending a city, ch217). You can re-climb only along the path you already took, faster than the first time. A **severing elixir** deletes that path so you can rebuild from scratch with a better foundation - at the cost of the easy way back (ch206, 217).
- [S] **Triple Block**: having three spirit abilities in the soul multiplies the latent-aura requirement for ascension by 100. Publicly nobody has ever broken through; theory says it's possible and may carry a benefit (ch209). Triple-blocked two-stars are common in the arena's ranked scene (ch214).
- [S] Two-stars have enhanced cognition ("two-star brain") and body control; the protagonist can copy any movement he perceives (ch221).
- [S] Archhumans outlive mortal lifespans by a lot (a judge born ~20 years after the Rift is active in 2220) (ch225).

### Becoming an archhuman
- [S] Mortals become archhumans by obtaining an affinity **prime**; primes are bought, and desirable ones (dream affinity -> white-collar careers) are expensive enough that families save for years (ch215).

### Energy
- [S] **Essence** is the spendable resource, talked about as a % of one's pool; stored in the stars (a diagnostic drains "the stars" empty, ch211). Ability costs are quoted in % essence (e.g. a set bonus that triggers on abilities costing under 5%) (ch220).
- [S] **Reflux essence**: using abilities or raw manipulation leaves residue in the body, tracked per affinity as a %. It dissipates slowly; a tempering technique (Purifying Crimson Spring) speeds it (ch204). [I] Reflux is the true limiter on sustained casting.
- [S] Essence supply is called the most important factor in long-term growth: more essence = more ability practice = less need for danger, and more income (ch204).
- [S] **Raw essence manipulation** (shaping your affinity's material without an ability) is always available, weak, and gentle on a damaged soul (ch204, 216, 217).
- [S] **Ether** is the ambient medium; regions have ether density (Faralethal very high; the Northern Belt is an "ether desert") (ch207, 217). "Ethertech" = engineered devices (drones, phones, BC) (ch216).
- [S] **Aura/presence**: higher stars exert physical-feeling pressure; a five-star's is like carrying a mountain for a two-star (ch205). Flaring aura in public is rude or illegal where mortals and weak one-stars live; in Starhold everyone flares to set pecking order (ch208). Essence signatures are unique and used for authentication (medals, official tokens) (ch206, 210).

### Status "screen"
- [S] There is NO system UI in these chapters. The protagonist keeps a **handwritten notebook** (ch204). Its layout:
  - GATHERING: star + readiness; "N-star satellite"
  - TALENT: name: quality + type ("Dynamic-quality healing")
  - TRUE SOUL CONSTRUCT: name, then effect list with % values
  - TEMPERING TECHNIQUES, grouped by affinity: "Stage N - X% Progress" (or "Stage 1 - Complete")
  - ACTIVE ABILITIES, grouped by affinity: same stage/progress format
  - CURSED ITEMS: name: effect
- [S] Item lines look like: "Chaos Hammer: +8% hearing acuity, Chaotic Essence Burst (29% efficiency)" (ch221).
- [S] The only machine-style prompts are on the imperial training-dummy control panel (ch224) and the soul-therapy screen (ch211).

### Abilities and tempering techniques
- [S] Two tracks per affinity: **tempering techniques** (passive body/essence cultivation, e.g. Sea of Blood, Crimson Mercury, Water Body) and **active abilities**. Each has a **Stage** and a 0-100% progress bar (ch204). At ascension-ready second star, nearly everything sits at Stage 3 / 0%; a Lord-tier fighter uses a Stage 4 ability (ch213). [I] Stage cap = star count + 1, unlocking on ascension.
- [S] A new hybrid affinity starts at Stage 0 (perished water: 52%) (ch204).
- [S] Abilities gain **named upgrades** as they grow: "tension" (Hydraulic Throw - charge indefinitely, even until the arm breaks), "anti-gravity" (Blood Javelin), "acceleration" (Accelerate Blood Projectile) (ch210).
- [S] Power/damage is benchmarked against known abilities: an item "negates about a mid-Stage-1 Tectonic Strike"; an item effect runs at "29% efficiency" relative to a Stage 0 / 0% spell of the same affinity and essence cost (ch219, 221).
- [S] Efficiency vs spectacle: efficient abilities are visually plain; flashiness is wasted essence (ch214).
- [S] Abilities are "mentally selected"; spirit abilities are spoken aloud (ch203, 214).

### Talents
- [S] One talent is normal; "double-talented" people exist and are notable (ch208). Talents have quality grades - "dynamic-quality" and "supreme-quality" healing are named (ch204, 218). Talents can be upgraded later (ch221 mentions a possible future "talent upgrade").
- [S] People routinely under-report what their talent does (ch216). Examples seen: see-past-states vision sold as "True Sight"; manipulation talents; speed talents; spectral extra hands; a life-affinity talent that reset a man's biological age to zero.
- [S] A target can sometimes feel a talent being used on them (ch216).

### Body
- [S] Body-enhancing drugs/steroids exist with serious side effects; the protagonist abused them under cover of his healing and has freakish strength and perception (ch206, 218). Not even supreme healing undoes shifts in brain chemistry (ch218).
- [S] Clinics sell body modification and "tempering aid" (ch211). Life-affinity practitioners need your consent to let their essence into your body - and could do terrible things once in (ch221).

## 2. Soul, soul damage, soul healing

- [S] The soul holds powers AND is the seat of choice and subjective experience (ch211). Its inner space is the **ethercosm**, where stars, cracks and a caged bonded spirit are visible (ch204, 212).
- [S] Cause here: offering the soul to an **Unholy Blessing** (ch208, 211).
- [S] Symptoms: emotional flatness (the emotions still fire; the ability to *experience* them is what's broken, ch212), lost hours/days, fainting when overtaxed, agony when using abilities (ch203-207). Spirit abilities and raw manipulation remain usable (ch217). Ascension is blocked until healed (ch204).
- [S] Hard scars never self-heal; softer damage does. Healing is driven by **significant lived experience** - good or bad; grief healed more than fun did. Healing causes headaches (ch207, 215, 219).
- [S] Why healing talents can't fix it: the soul stores the body's template, so healing rebuilds the body toward that image; the only "template" of a soul is the person's whole lived identity, which is too fluid to copy. Recovery = re-embodying and discovering who you are (ch212).
- [S] **Soul elixirs** add blank, pluripotent soul-tissue. Fine for minor damage; for severe damage they effectively produce a different person over time (ch212). Most patients give up and drink one anyway (ch219).
- [S] Cracks that set in bad shapes yield monstrous drives; first-stage therapy (45 days: identity affirmations under amnestics, sensory flooding, an emotional-support creature called a goom) steers them (ch211-212). Then fortnightly sessions; passive estimate ~6 years, cut by months per major life event; no upper bound if you keep re-injuring (ch219). Sleep does nothing for the soul; waking experience does (ch221).
- [S] **Infohazard**: how soul damage/healing works is illegal to publish because knowing it endangers the patient (ch207, 211).
- [S] **Soul-stabilizing Ring** (prototype): lets a soul-damaged person use normal abilities for ~8 s once per day; more risks permanent damage; theoretical max ~13 s (ch223-224).
- [S] Arena simulation causes no soul consequences (ch219).

## 3. The Lifesteal talent

- [S] Sheet entry: "1% Lifesteal: Dynamic-quality healing" (ch204). [I] He heals from damage/kills he inflicts; a summon's kills also heal him (ch218).
- [S] Officials describe the result as biological immortality plus reversal of any bodily injury, soul excepted (ch209). It even nullified a congenital terminal illness (ch215). Body returns to a baseline self-image (face/hair restored, ch203).
- [S] Survives loss of most of the body via a "spark of undeath" state he has to fight through until regenerated; a past state of him was literally just a central nervous system (ch216, 221). Head/brain destruction is still the implied kill condition (dummies "avoid the brain", ch225).
- [S] Limits: no soul healing; no reversal of neurochemical changes (ch218); muscles can be drained of strength and physique can waste (ch217); he can still be eaten while unconscious (ch221); "you're tough, not immortal" - a deviant with the wrong talent could counter him (ch218).
- Known exploits [S]:
  - Health as ammunition: overcharge attacks until his own limbs break (ch210); sacrifice limbs to Unholy Blessing (ch210); feed lost blood to Sanguine Avenger for retaliation hits (ch225).
  - Ignore all non-lethal damage and trade hits; multi-day nonstop fighting (ch216); 7 dungeons a day where once a week is normal (ch223).
  - Tank drug side effects for permanent physical gains (ch206, 218).
  - Organ farming: regrown organs carry his supernatural qualities and sold well enough to back a $270B advance - until a conglomerate cloned them (ch206, 217).
  - Combined with the Cycle Scythe: kills refund essence and purge reflux -> indefinite uptime (ch204).

## 4. True soul construct and bonded spirit

### Soul constructs
- [S] People can have a **soul construct**; his original one refunded 1% essence per kill (ch204). A construct can be **awakened** into a "true" one using a **prime remnant**, which exists only in primordial domains and takes a long chain of trials to win. Almost nobody knows this; a judge doubts even the empress has done it (ch224).
- [S] **Sentient ether constructs cannot lie**; they're rarely good fighters unless combat is their core concept (ch203, 218).

### Cycle Scythe (a.k.a. Cycle Rose)
- [S] Rests as a black coiling tattoo with golden lips; manifests as a scythe that bleeds essence-blood and talks in many voices - it voices the owner's subconscious, and only explains an effect after he discovers it himself (ch203-204, 206).
- [S] It is a **weapon modifier**: wraps any object the user can demonstrably wield as a weapon (fist, shoe, rock, uprooted tree, bought sword, conjured blood weapon). **Wield Assist** = a "soul muscle" carries the weight; no weight cap except user strength, but overload exhausts it -> forced retract, ~5 min recovery (ch203).
- [S] **Essence Extraction**: per kill, +1% essence and just under -1% reflux on every affinity (ch204). He hides this for fear of conscription.
- [S] Eats up to **two cursed items: one regular + one unique**; their effects mutate toward weapon-modding and get stronger by being focused on one weapon and by consuming the item's unused potential (ch204). Current: Weapon Empowerment +37% damage/+37% durability; **Omnibleed (Moderate)** - anything bleeds, objects lose durability over time (armor included, ch218), creatures lose more than blood; Blood Synergy +79% to blood weapons' special abilities (also boosted a bought hammer's blood-essence burst, ch221); Bloodshed Bond (unknown).
- [S] Scales with gathering; large jumps per star (ch204).

### Bloodshed
- [S] A bonded spirit (blood skeleton) summoned via the spirit ability **Blood Sacrifice**; size scales with blood supplied; no permanent cost. Even small it hits like a competent three-star caster: explosive blood bolts, drinks corpse blood to refuel, cleaver melee, binding tendrils, real tactical skill (ch218). Its kills are credited to the summoner for war records (ch210) and heal him (ch218).
- [S] Lives in a cage in his soul; can step out; secretly shielded - and then tweaked - one soul crack tied to his drive for slaughter during therapy (ch212). "Releasing" it (vs summoning) risks permanent death (ch204).

## 5. Abilities / affinities catalogue (as seen)

Affinities named: blood, water, perished water (hybrid), life, dream, light, wind/air, metal, fire, earth, lightning, miasma, poison, plus a unique "fusion" affinity (ch204-221).
- Blood: Terror Knuckles (cheap spikes), Blood Javelin, Absorb Blood, Accelerate Blood Projectile, Sanguine Avenger (conjured greatsword; drinks the user's spilled blood to power a retaliation strike, ch225), Knuckle Blade, Crimson Wing Shield (forearm shields), Dissipate Wave, Crimson Dragon Scales (armor). Tempering: Sea of Blood (huge internal blood reserve), Crimson Mercury ("blood metal"; can be accelerated but not while held, ch214), Purifying Crimson Spring (clears reflux).
- Water: Tsunami Strike (big risky hit), Hydraulic Flex (burst movement), Create Water, Hydraulic Throw. Tempering: Adaptive Water Body, Hundred Thousand Wet Hells, Abyssal Depths, Water Body.
- Perished water: Perished Water, Ghosts of the Drowned (Stage 0). Its orb colour (deep blue) is something he prefers hidden (ch211).
- Others: Floating Leaf (wind movement), metal push/pull on held metal (can ride metal at huge cost), Eagle Eyes (perception tempering), Tectonic Strike (earth), crystallized miasma shards (rot flesh on skin contact, useless vs full armor), invisibility (light).
- Spirit abilities [S]: spoken to trigger; usually carry costs; cost-free ones are rare and sell for fortunes (ch225) -> they are tradeable acquisitions. Max three before Triple Block.
  - Twisted Pendulum: ~10 s penalty where the user is slowed, then ~5 s real time at 2x subjective speed; re-castable (ch214, 222, 225).
  - Blood Sacrifice (summon Bloodshed); Leviathan's Fury (spectral leviathan jaws, ch221).
  - Unholy Blessing: sacrifice a body part (limb) or soul for a burst of power (ch208, 210). (Unclear if this is a spirit ability or another category.)
  - Arena examples: massive regen that burns your life; drop a star for strength; minor fatigue for power + radiant glow (ch214).
- Consumable combat boosts: burnout serum (ch208), inhaled sprays (ch214).
- Teleportation is rare; most dramatic exits are stealth tricks (ch206).

## 6. Interspace, realms, dungeons

- [S] **Passages** open into the **interspace**, a web of **realms** joined by more passages; depth is counted in "steps". Realms have letter classes (B-class, C-class seen) and sizes; passages vary from football-sized to double doors (ch203, 207, 221).
- [S] Topology is non-Euclidean: a few steps in may exit across the planet. Useful chains become **portal hubs** (armored tunnels, carts/pods, fees, queues) (ch207, 215). One door-sized passage in Pittersville leads to another planet, **Faralethal**; traffic is shot through in pods at speeds dangerous below second star (ch207).
- [S] Realms can be purged and developed: a C-class dead-end became a no-status-display resort ($100k/month), another houses the empire's main training city (ch221, 224).
- [S] Cities sit on or near passages and suffer periodic **monster invasions**; Starhold faces a permanent tide from Faralethal's countless passages (ch207, 216).
- [S] **Wild interspace** vs **dungeons**: wild interspace near high-ether zones is lethal but the best money; tame in ether deserts (ch217).
- [S] **Dungeons = anomalous realms**. They conjure false people/monsters (monsters really do spawn from nothing there, ch216); "nothing but the reward is real" (ch218). Types named:
  - **Resetting dungeons**: periodically restored to an identical state. Normally nothing survives removal except the designated reward. **Resource caches** let one resource type (ore, lumber, stone, elixir fruit) - sometimes everything - be carried out every reset = effectively infinite supply (ch221).
  - **Instance dungeons** (incl. unexplored "mystery" instances that factions pay suicidal bounties to scout) (ch221).
  - Scenario dungeons: Fortress Dungeon (defend), Mountain Pass (assault), classic chambers-then-boss (rated for 20 people), trap mazes (Shortish's Purple Maze needs a scout) (ch216, 218, 221).
  - Trial/prison dungeons: the Century of Solitude (a century of subjective time, a madness entity, a three-way reward choice at the end) (ch203, 207, 220).
  - The **Tower of York**: spots are auctioned; earlier entrants do better (ch221).
- [S] Rules observed: party caps (5; 20); entrance seals when the party crosses a marker; minimum progress to unlock the exit (5 of 10 walls); escalating stages each with troops + elites + commander/boss; big spike past the midpoint; enemies adapt gear (ch218). Hazard environments need airlocks (toxic fog) (ch221).
- [S] **Rewards** appear per person at the exit, scaled to personal contribution and difficulty - a passenger got a few silver coins while the carry got a $1.5B bracelet (ch218). Extraordinary feats can yield unique items and may permanently close a dungeon (ch217). Some dungeons are once-per-person; repeat clears give diminishing rewards (ch221).
- [S] Dungeons are rated by rank and party size ("advanced two-star party"). Certification counts "full completions of dungeons suitable for your rank" (ch221).
- [S] **Access economy**: near Starhold most good passages are faction-owned; owners sell **tickets**, mostly by auction (the ATA does not sell access). Auction houses apply **bidding bonuses** to members/allies (+300% seen; a neutral house caps at +80%; a hostile house gave him -50%); the empress's platinum Tower of York cards give +20%. Permanent bonuses can be earned through near-suicidal contract jobs. Brokers refuse clients who would antagonize big factions. Tickets also change hands as arena wagers (ch216, 221). In the Northern Belt ownership rules are looser (ch218).
- [S] Typical numbers: mid two-star dungeon ~ $6M profit/clear; poison-fruit cache ~ $3M/run (fruits worth thousands each); a once-only boss dungeon dropped a $50M sword (ch221).
- [S] Safety norm: more than one delve a week is considered too much; more than three is insane (ch223). Party splits are negotiated percentages (35% scout share; 70/30) (ch216, 217).
- "Safety levels": the only explicit safety-level scale in these chapters is on imperial training automatons (high ... none) (ch224). Realm/dungeon danger is otherwise expressed by letter class and recommended rank/party.
- What happens if passages are NOT cleared: not spelled out here beyond invasions and the endless Faralethal tide; [I] uncontrolled passages leak monsters.

## 7. Monsters / enemies

- [S] Monsters are rated by star-equivalent strength; "giant-class" is a size/threat class; **deviants** are monsters with freak talents; named types: quasi-minotaur, deviant golem, undead giant, stone golem (shrapnel blast), clawed ape, fire-breathing tiger, invisible light-affinity stalkers, dog-sized rodents, flesh abominations guarding resources, skull-headed demon boss (ch203, 210, 216, 221).
- [S] Dungeon humanoids: infantry, archers, ballista crews, elites, commanders; use shield walls and formations at high tiers; show despair when losing (ch218).
- [S] Corpses have salvage value tallied by collection companies/watchmen (ch209, 216).
- [S] Imperial **practice automatons**: impact-alloy bodies layered in enchantments; configurable count, rank level, style (ranged / defensive melee / offensive melee), safety; they coordinate as a party; joint lights go green -> yellow -> orange -> red -> dark (ch224-225). Their crossbows hit enormously hard but have brutal recoil and per-shot cost.

## 8. Items and tech

- [S] "Cursed items" = "artifacts". Most have ONE effect, usually <=10% (ability power, essence discount, a physical attribute). Examples: Ocean Medallion -13% water essence cost; a moderate-strength sword ($50M); defensive bracelet ($1.5B: every ~5 h negate about a mid-Stage-1 Tectonic Strike of damage) (ch204, 219, 221).
- [S] **Sets**: the standard is three items bound into a set with a set bonus (temporary buffs, extra bleed, armor weakening, paralysis, doubled projectiles for <5%-cost abilities; at the top: a "primary ability" pick for -35% cost/+19% power (~$100B); 97% discount on manipulating metal objects under 300 g ($130B)) (ch220).
- [S] **Gem of Binding** creates sets. Natural gems are dungeon rewards; a **Superior** one binds four. The factory-made **Inferior Gem of Binding** ($6,240,200,000): three items, no uniques, single effects <=10%, "sister concepts" only, needs a master artificer, <=90% success (ch220).
- [S] **Unique** items grow: they are "open" to aura thrown off by events matching their growth condition. Cheap uniques have bad build quality (upkeep), weak effects, or awkward growth conditions. Chaos Hammer went +8% -> +41% hearing and 29% -> 312% burst efficiency in a year of heavy use, then plateaued (diminishing returns). A +2% all-physical ring (speed, strength, durability, endurance, perception) grows on the wearer's physical improvement but needs an improvement big enough to be a world "event" (ch220-221). Items have **concepts** (chaos, bloodshed...) that shape effects.
- [S] S-grade uniques are auction-only (ch220). Equipment can be lost/destroyed (ch210). A "$100M gear set" is low-end for an elite two-star (ch218).
- [S] Utility: storage rings (owner-synced), Ether Capture Ring (mints coins of pure aura; not yet public), food pills, sleep-compression pills, essence-restoring cocktails, amnestics, noise-cancelling necklaces, privacy-enchanted screens, spatially expanded interiors (carriages, flats, buildings), flying carriages/platforms, recording crystals, drones (ch203-223).
- [S] Tech level: post-collapse rebuild - brick phones and tablets are new luxuries, BC broadcast sets are state-subsidized, cars are armored and unlicensed (ch211, 216, 221).
- [S] Crafting roles named: artificers (set fusion), appraisers, elixir makers using dungeon-grown ingredients (ch220-221). No inscription/crystal crafting detail appears in these chapters beyond "enchantments" and "recording crystal".

## 9. Economy and price points (US dollars)

- Street level: thousand-dollar bills exist; a stack of them buys a day in a slum flat (ch215).
- Starhold: $70k lift ride; $100k/month resort; ~$1B/year university access plus class fees; $11B luxury flat; floating estates out of reach at ~$40B liquid; $14M/hour arena VVIP lounge (-10% for ten hours) ~ ten buildings elsewhere (ch211-221).
- Items: B list $1-5B, A list $5-10B, S list $10B+; top sets $100-130B (ch220).
- Income: $3-6M per mid dungeon; 1,000+ solo clears plus sales took him from ~$30B to ~$100B in a year (ch221). Organ contract balance $270B (ch217).
- Institutions: **ATA** (Archhumanity's Trading Association - empress-owned, regulated marketplace, appraisal, auctions, concierge for special-rank clients; first ten appraisals free; service tiers by badge rank) (ch219-220); faction auction houses; collection companies; banks that issue wealth badges; conglomerates (Pale Moon Industries / Severe Clan; Golden Sword).
- Big money = Faralethal expedition licenses and slave-worked ore mines (ch213).

## 10. Reputation: medals and badges

- [S] Ranks/colours: first = bronze, second = dull silver, third = gold, fourth = platinum, fifth = violet, **special** = white with a unique engraved scene and an S (ch210, 221).
- [S] War medals (the empire counts monster-slaying as service in its "Eternal War"): Trooper (kills; >1M before third star = fifth), Slayer (kills of above-second-star monsters; 720+ as a two-star = special), Giantslayer (one-shotting a giant-class as a two-star = special), Veteran (time in live combat; 3+ months = second), Expert (battles survived: <10 first, 10+ second, 100+ third, ~250 next), Martyr (usually posthumous) (ch210).
- [S] Civil tracks: Dungeoneer and Solo Dungeoneer (250 full clears = third / fourth respectively; 1,000 = fourth / fifth), wealth, business, academics, delving, exploring, sports, arts (third-rank Virtuoso Violinist on debut was scandalously high) (ch210, 221, 222). No badges for delving *speed*, deliberately (ch223).
- [S] Mechanics: minted with the owner's essence signature, radiate aura, cling to clothing via aura, self-destruct in a foreign storage ring; theft = execution (ch210). Audited - an implausible record gets you detained (comfortably) while every clear is verified, then observed re-runs (ch224).
- [S] Effects: crowds defer to a fifth-rank; service doors/catalogues unlock by rank; special-rank holders are treated like nobility by state bodies; war medals get medical bills paid by the treasury; badges act as a CV. Hiding then flashing badges to humiliate is a punishable faux pas. "Imperial Accomplishments" (badges) are also the currency for political asks - e.g. expanding a subordinate territory's rights (ch209-211, 219-220, 224).

## 11. Society, law, factions

- [S] Setting: post-**Rift** "New Earth", 2218-2220; the (American) Empire under an empress; capital functions shifting to Starhold on Faralethal (ch207-208, 222, 225).
- [S] Default state of war with everyone and everything - the **Eternal War** (ch209).
- [S] **Lordship Qualification Standard**: personal power is essentially the only criterion for holding land; cruelty is tolerated because Lords are what keeps cities alive (ch225). Adjudicators can deed cities on potential; two-stars almost never get fiefs; the Council of Imperial Adjudicators (three four-star judges, majority vote, rigid speaking order) can confiscate. Lordship can be stripped as a mild penalty (ch208-209, 213).
- [S] **Subordinate territories** (Northern Belt) have reduced rights; integration is possible. Repentawa ended up under direct imperial supervision with a locally elected council - elections are rare (ch209).
- [S] The empire is itself a faction with an army: many four-star adjudicators and three-star "watchdogs", individually weaker than faction elites but numerous (ch224). No direct orders to private archhumans outside emergencies (ch224).
- [S] Great clans: Umbra (neutral assassin family, Overlord head; political engagements), Kraven (ruined), Severe (Pale Moon), Lavizzi; guild Valhalla in the North; fixer Basilisk, whose contracts and supply chains are rigged to collapse if his killer lives (ch205, 213, 217).
- [S] Law favours money ("everything is legal with enough lawyers"); killings in petty archhuman feuds are routine; the state doesn't stop self-endangerment; trespassing in the council building is capital (ch205, 208, 216).
- [S] Ideology: keep the dream of social mobility alive via media (talk shows, arena, frontier footage); hardship is believed to produce the rare Overlord-grade people; easy paths produce many mediocre 2-3 stars (ch213). Archhumans widely regard mortals as lesser (ch222). People who outshine the throne tend to come to harm (ch218, 223).
- [S] Status etiquette: superiors don't approach inferiors; Starhold is mostly peak two-stars and three-stars; slavery exists in mines (ch208, 213, 223).
- [S] Education: academies (<=10 years), universities (per-professor classes, paywalled), concept libraries that sell knowledge (ch219, 224).
- Contracts: the organ deal was repayable only from organ sales, so the debt died with the market (ch217). Magical oaths are not shown in these chapters.

## 14. Combat texture

- Fights between peers are decided in seconds; openings, risk tolerance and stacking multipliers matter more than raw stats (ch214, 225).
- Signature kill = stacking: time dilation + limb sacrifice + overcharged throw + projectile mods + item effect (ch210).
- Attrition style: accept every non-lethal wound, bleed the target (even armor) with Omnibleed, heal off the damage dealt (ch216, 218).
- Party play: tank/shield, ranged, scout roles; reading enemy reload cycles; using battlefield debris; "treat every fight seriously" (ch218). Ordinary fighters fear even bruises and need recovery time between delves (ch218, 223).
- Armor counters flesh-only affinities (miasma) (ch218). Invisible enemies need counters (ch216).
- Injury is graphic and location-based (severed joints, shattered jaw); healers can save most things short of brain destruction (ch225).
- Spirit-ability penalties create timing play: cast during a countdown so the penalty expires as the fight starts (ch221, 225).

## 18. Design hooks (my suggestions, not canon)

- Core loop candidate: damage-dealt heals you; kills refund 1% resource and purge 1% "reflux" heat -> aggression sustains uptime.
- Health-as-ammo: overcharge attacks that break your own limbs; blood lost powers a retaliation meter.
- Weapon-modifier companion that wraps anything you can lift, eats one regular + one unique item, only reveals effects after you discover them, and heckles you.
- Reflux per affinity as a soft cooldown/heat system; Triple Block as a build trade-off (three powerful spirit abilities vs 100x rank-up cost).
- Dungeon structures: N-wall assault with clear-at-least-half exit rule; resetting resource caches; once-only boss rooms; diminishing rewards on repeats; contribution-scaled personal loot.
- Meta layer: ticket auctions with faction bidding bonuses/penalties; badge ranks (bronze-silver-gold-platinum-violet-white) unlocking vendors; unique items that grow by condition with diminishing returns; three-item sets via binding gems with a 90% fusion chance.
- Soul damage as a long-term debuff healed by "significant events", with a fast-but-identity-altering elixir shortcut.
- Arena mode with no consequences and no progression credit.
