# 1% Lifesteal — Mechanics notes, Chapters 1–6 (public intro on Royal Road)

Paraphrased working notes for game-design reference. No source text is stored here.
Tags: **[S]** = stated outright in the text, **[I]** = my inference. (ch N) = chapter.

## 1. World frame
- [S] Setting is "New Earth", ~200 years after an event called **the Rift**. The Rift's anniversary is a public holiday. (ch 1, 6)
- [S] The Rift **changed physics**. Pre-Rift tech (cars, TVs, computers, planes) stopped working; most of it has since been re-created by other means under the new rules. A mystical energy, **ether**, is what the new world runs on. (ch 2)
- [S] Everyday tech is **crystal + inscription** based: broadcast crystals ("BC", TV equivalent), comm crystals, inscribed metal scanners at tills, pay-per-message stone "contact tablets" in building lobbies, light crystals, crystal-surface food/drink dispensers in rich offices, portable screens with auction listings. (ch 1–4)
- [S] Transport: drake-drawn carriages and ridden drakes for ordinary people; rare self-propelled carriages; sky bridges and underground transit for the rich. **Space dilation** (bigger on the inside) is common in wealthy/official contexts — carriages, rooms, storage "from nowhere". (ch 1–3)
- [S] Currency is dollars. Banks, taxes, insurance (incl. break insurance tied to district of residence) all exist. (ch 2, 5)
- [S] Cities are split into numbered districts with brutal wealth stratification; the rich district has floating islands, castles, orbital gardens. Districts can be made private: "mortals" need ID/business to enter. (ch 1, 3)

## 2. People: mortals vs archhumans
- [S] Humans who have taken a talent into their soul are **archhumans ("archs")**; everyone else is legally and socially a **mortal**. (ch 1–5)
- [S] Archs get a distinct ID card. It lists **affinity**, not talent — talents are treated as private, partly because political factions want members' capabilities secret. Registration is a multi-office bureaucratic slog; a poor registrant triggers a police check on where the prime came from. Arch-facing offices run 24/7. (ch 5)
- [S] Archs can live well past a century. (ch 1)
- [S] Rank is counted in **stars in the soul**. 1-star: barely different from a mortal, no detectable presence. 2-star and up: visible physical changes (odd hair/eye colours, unnatural beauty, size) and an **aura** that presses on anyone lower-ranked — nausea, dizziness, dread — unless the owner suppresses it. Stars are the main class marker but not a strict measure of power. (ch 3, 5)
- [S] A strong arch can push a calming/commanding pulse onto a weaker person with a word. (ch 4)
- [S] Independent archs without backing have very high death and early-retirement rates. Healing (mortal medicine and especially healer services) is extremely expensive. (ch 4)
- [S] **Talents are permanent.** Once embraced there is no swapping. (ch 4)
- [S] Talents are expected to **evolve as the holder ranks up**. (ch 4)
- [S] Monsters also carry stars in their souls; ordinary animals do not. (ch 6)

## 3. Vestiges (how talents exist in the world)
- [S] A **prime vestige** is a small talking orb with a face and a personality. It holds one talent plus one or more elemental affinities. (ch 2–4)
- [S] Mortals can **manifest** a prime spontaneously; a violent encounter with monsters sharply raises the odds but never guarantees it (otherwise people would farm primes by throwing mortals at weak monsters). Freddy's appeared the morning after his first kill. (ch 2, 3)
- [S] A prime **must answer truthfully if it knows the answer**. Standard questions: "what power do you hold?" and "what affinities do you have?". It may answer cryptically, say it doesn't know, or stay silent on edge cases. (ch 3, 4)
- [S] To use one: state the intent to accept its power into your soul; it agrees, turns to mist, and sinks into the body. You wake up an arch. (ch 4)
- [S] Primes are tradeable property. Rowdy ones are carried in sound-insulating glass boxes. (ch 3)
- [S] Valuation drivers: combat vs non-combat talent; how restrictive the talent is; **number of affinities** (three is rare and very valuable). A cheap prime costs six figures; a non-combat triple-affinity prime was listed at auction around $70M. A formal **appraisal** costs at least a cheap prime's worth and can take ~6 months without contacts. (ch 3, 4)
- [S] Other vestige classes are named: **wild vestiges** (live in the Netherecho, embody the concept of an object/place — an alarm clock obsessed with hurrying, a chest that hoards secrets, a window that hates the sun, a weeping glass of water), **unique vestiges**, and **remnants** (rarer still). (ch 6)
- [S] Objects soaked in blood from a slaughter **caused by a passage** become **catalysts** — they carry power that cannot be manufactured, and are collected after a break. A unique can ride a vessel (e.g. a victim's bloodied clothes) away from the scene and manifest later. (ch 6)
- [I] Freddy's bean bag was the catalyst of a unique/remnant; the bloody skeleton entity behind his bin that calls him "Master" is that vestige. His own red 1% Lifesteal prime was bought, not manifested. (ch 5, 6)

## 4. The protagonist's talent
- [S] Vestige's self-description, paraphrased: deal a hundred parts of harm to your enemy and you recover one part. The trade name is **1% Lifesteal**; its affinity is **water** (single). (ch 4)
- [S] Considered a strong *budget* talent for an unbacked arch because it offsets healing costs. (ch 4)
- [S] Its real rules are **unknown and must be found by experiment** — the consultant lists open questions: who counts as an "enemy" (your view or theirs?); what "recover" covers (wounds, disease, poison, scars, lost limbs, cancer?); 1% of what (overkill vs lethal pinpoint strike — same heal?); instant or over time; does indirect harm count (fire you set, bleeding you caused)? "Healing quality" has known categories and a talent can also sit off the normal scale. Such talents are only as good as their technicalities. (ch 4)

## 5. Ether, affinities and the soul
- [S] **Ether** takes on the nature of whatever it touches — the **ether shift**. Shifted particles are **wisps**, the most common form of attuned ether. Collecting wisps is **gathering**; several methods exist. (ch 6)
- [S] The **ethercosm** is the inner projection of the soul: an almost empty dark space containing your **stars**. A new arch's first star is pure white = raw/unattuned = useless essence, "an empty cup". It is activated by grabbing a first wisp of your affinity. (ch 6)
- [S] The **Netherecho** is a deeper layer of reality made of truths/concepts, entered by projecting the soul out of the ethercosm through an imagined exit. Real objects appear as painted caricatures; wisps drift as coloured paint splotches behaving like their element; your projection is tiny and fragile. **If the projection is destroyed, you die.** Wild vestiges there can turn hostile without warning. Skilled people can inspect an item's history/nature there. (ch 6)
- [S] Affinities seen so far: water, earth, nature, (fire implied via fire elementals). Affinity drives where you should live and hunt: water archs grow reliably near big bodies of water, earn well in deserts, and have an easy time against fire elementals. (ch 3, 4)

## 6. Abilities and how they are learned
- [S] **Ether-imprint scrolls**: sealed with inscribed tape; looking at the contents writes the ability into your soul. (ch 4)
- [S] Ability categories shown in the starter kit:
  - **Mobility skill** — Frog Leap (hydraulic pressure for movement).
  - **Martial art package** — Flowing Rain: bundles a **tempering technique** (Water Body: generic water body-tempering, good recovery/health, can evolve into a vitality booster) and a **strike technique** (Flowing Strike: uses momentum of water in the body; hitting solid targets before the body adapts ruptures your own vessels). A better tempering technique (Flowing River) is sold separately.
  - **Spell** — Squirt: a throwaway training spell that teaches materialising water from essence and manipulating it; you are expected to then **build your own** Create Water spell.
  - **Manual** — The Basics of Gathering, affinity-specific.
- [I] So: abilities are learnable by anyone with the right affinity, improve through bodily adaptation, evolve, and can be self-authored once the principle is understood. Talent is the one fixed, unique piece.

## 7. Passages, realms, breaks
- [S] **Passages** are portals to other **realms**. Realms have catalogue codes (e.g. C-000421), can be many Earths in size, and have many entrances. A passage has a **step count**; a **zero-step passage** links a realm directly to New Earth and is a major political/economic event. (ch 6)
- [S] A **break** is when a passage's inhabitants pour out into the city. Trained arch responders clear it; officials then take witness reports. Mortals are **not paid for kills** during a break — they are supposed to evacuate. Kill gear from invaders is confiscated. (ch 2)
- [S] Passages are harvested for exotic flora and more; the rich show off passage plants. Known passages are sealed behind reinforced, access-controlled doors. (ch 1, 6)
- [S] First enemy type: small masked, cloaked humanoids with serrated daggers, a supernaturally fast dash with a telltale sound, leathery-tough bodies, their own language and a revenge ethic. One realm shown: endless golden grassland with aerial and burrowing predators. (ch 2, 6)
- [S] On a mortal's first monster kill: burning in the torso, dizziness, voices, then it "pops". (ch 2) [I] this is ether/essence intake.

## 8. Power structure
- [S] An **empire** governs; it is not unified — parties, clans and organisations compete. **City lords** effectively own cities; clan heads can be their hierarchical equals. Dealings between them are governed by **contracts** with enforceable clauses (e.g. who may retrieve items manifested from an incident). (ch 5, 6)
- [S] **Archhumanity's Trading Association (ATA)**: the marketplace for primes and more. Tiered, numbered doors by client importance; floating golden surveillance orbs; theft on its grounds is lethal; accusing it of scamming is dangerous; it auctions primes publicly. Bundles "consulting" (starter scrolls + advice). (ch 3, 4)
- [S] "Archs United" is named as a founding-era organisation. (ch 6)
- [S] A blood-manipulating clan (red robes, facial markings) collects break catalysts. (ch 6)

## 9. Tone rules (matter for a faithful game)
- Grimdark, class rage, gallows humour; money pressure is constant and concrete (prices are always given).
- Power is earned through pain, self-injury and grind; knowledge is paywalled and everyone is trying to fleece the newcomer.
- Trauma is persistent, not cured.
