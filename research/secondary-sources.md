# 1% Lifesteal - Secondary-Source Research

Compiled 2026-09-18. Everything below is paraphrased from PUBLIC secondary sources (fan wiki, TV Tropes, retailer/publisher listings, reviews, trade press). No book text was read or copied; no piracy/aggregator sites were visited. Royal Road and Patreon could not be fetched directly (403 / blocked), so anything attributed to them comes from search-result snippets only.

Confidence key:
- **HIGH** - stated by publisher/retailer, or stated consistently by two or more independent fan sources.
- **MED** - stated by one fan source (wiki or TV Tropes) with no contradiction.
- **LOW** - fan speculation, a single reviewer's impression, or my own inference.

## 1. Power system and world rules

### 1.1 CONFIRMED (multiple sources or publisher-stated)

| Mechanic | Paraphrased description | Source(s) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setting | "New Earth", about 200 years after a reality-altering event (fans call it "The Rift") when passages to other realms opened and the laws of physics changed. Grim, stratified, might-makes-right society. | [AETHON], [PFDB], [TVT], [WIKI] Northern Belt page | HIGH |
| Technology | Pre-Rift electronics mostly stopped working because physics changed; society rebuilt equivalents with magic-tech. Cars/planes are historical curiosities. | [TVT] | MED |
| Archhumans ("Arch") | Humans who take a Prime Vestige into their soul. They gain a talent plus one or more elemental affinities, and their soul can grow past mortal limits. Non-arch people are called "mortals". Becoming one normally costs a fortune; the protagonist spent a decade saving for it. | [TVT], [WIKI], [GR], [AETHON] blurb | HIGH |
| Prime Vestige | A special ether construct that confers a talent. It appears when a mortal kills enough strong monsters, or when an archhuman kills a "deviant" monster. They are tradeable, brokered, and auctioned, with huge price spreads (protagonist got roughly $30k plus the Lifesteal vestige for one that later auctioned for about $75M). | [TVT], [WIKI] Freddy page, Gorel page | HIGH |
| Talent | The unique power granted by a Prime Vestige. Examples from the wiki: a health-tracking talent, lifespark manipulation, speed enhancement, the Empress's "Avatar of Dominion". Talents vary hugely in value; some are near-useless (a rat tail was offered as a trade). | [WIKI] character pages, [TVT] | HIGH |
| Affinities | Elemental/conceptual alignments that come with the Prime Vestige (usually one, sometimes more - more is worth more). They let the arch absorb matching "wisps" of ether to fuel the soul and power abilities. New affinities can appear on ascending a star (often related to existing ones; not guaranteed). Seen: water, blood, earth, metal, air, lightning, fire, life, death, plus hybrids ("life flame") and uniques ("Perished Water"). 1% Lifesteal itself carries water affinity. | [TVT], [WIKI] | HIGH |
| Stars (rank system) | Power is ranked in stars, 1 through 6. Four-star archs are city-lord / clan-head level; the Empress is 5-star; nobody human is known to have reached 6. Progress within a star is tracked as a percentage, with a bottleneck at 99% ("essence capacity" is shown as a % on the wiki progression sheet). | [WIKI] Power System, Ether constructs, Empress Kaiya; [GR]; [TVT] | HIGH |
| Ether and essence | Ether is the ambient magical medium; essence is the resource archs spend on abilities (ability and healing costs are quoted in essence). Essence is harvested from kills and in the Netherecho. | [WIKI], [TVT], [GR] | HIGH (that both exist) / MED (exact distinction) |
| Ether constructs | Concept-embodiments that live in the Netherecho. Five tiers: Vestige -> Remnant -> Spirit -> Eidolon -> Asura (the wiki also lists "wisps" below vestiges). They grow by absorbing ether or killing each other. They cannot lie. Spirits can briefly manifest physically; Eidolons exist permanently in both planes; Asura are almost never seen. Rough threat mapping: vestige ~ up to peak 1-star, remnant ~ 2 to early 3-star, spirit ~ up to early 4-star, eidolon ~ peak 4-star (some 5), asura ~ 5-6 star. | [WIKI] Ether constructs; [TVT] (agree on tier names and honesty rule) | HIGH (tiers), MED (threat mapping) |
| Ability stages | Abilities are built/upgraded by binding ether constructs into them: a vestige takes an ability from stage 0 to 1, a remnant 1 to 2, a spirit 2 to 3, an eidolon 3 to 4, (presumably) an asura 4 to 5. The concept of the construct used (e.g. "Acceleration", "Piercing", "Compression") shapes what the upgrade does. Abilities have a progress % toward the next stage. | [WIKI] Ether constructs, Freddy/Progression | MED-HIGH |
| Ability categories | (a) body-"tempering techniques" - passive, work by harming the body so it adapts; (b) active abilities, grouped by affinity; (c) spirit abilities. | [WIKI] Freddy/Progression; [TVT] | MED-HIGH |
| Spirit abilities | Rare, very strong abilities tied to a spirit-tier construct. Must be spoken aloud to trigger. Always carry a price (progression, limbs, life force, even the user's life) and/or strict trigger conditions. Each one slows soul growth substantially; fans explain this as "compressing" latent aura. In-story claim: three spirit abilities are required to pass 5 stars, which is why no human has. | [TVT]; [WIKI] Latent Aura Analogy | MED |
| The Netherecho | A parallel spiritual plane anchored to reality. Archs enter by meditating and projecting their soul. Only stationary (or repetitively moving) real-world things show up there. Damage there is soul damage. Perception range grows roughly 10 ft per star. Local ether mirrors the real place (water ether at sea, fire ether at a volcano). Archs can summon a "soul construct" there (the protagonist's is a scythe that speeds essence collection and absorbs items). One reviewer likened the Netherecho loop to a risk/reward minigame. | [WIKI] The Netherecho, Freddy/Progression; [TVT]; [GR] | HIGH (existence/entry), MED (details) |
| Passages / realms | Stable or wild portals to other realms, catalogued with codes (e.g. a "C-0004xx"-style designation). Realms have their own ecologies (the Gorel realm: underground hive creatures with short-lived queens and a periodic red-sky frenzy event). A "passage break" / "realm break" is when things spill out into Earth - this is the invasion mechanic and is how the protagonist's story starts. Discovering a new passage is a big, name-in-history event. | [TVT]; [WIKI] Northern Belt, Crimson Twilight, Gorel, Freddy | HIGH |
| Hubs | Commercial facilities built around passages. Example: a Nova York hub with free entry that takes half of whatever delvers bring out. | [WIKI] Santorio Hub | MED |
| Delving economy | "Delvers" work in parties, kill monsters, and haul whole corpses back because every part has a use. Carry capacity is a real economic stat. Indentured mining in clan-owned realms exists for debtors. | [TVT]; [AETHON] Book 2 blurb ("delver") | HIGH |
| Deviants | Variant monsters with off-pattern affinities/abilities; more dangerous, and the archhuman route to a Prime Vestige drop. | [TVT]; [WIKI] Gorel | HIGH |
| Dungeons | At least one "special passage" functions like a trial dungeon: one entrant at a time, extreme time dilation (about a century inside per hour outside), overseen by a spirit (of Insanity), with rewards for surviving. The wiki calls it "Insanity's dungeon". | [TVT]; [WIKI] Freddy infobox | MED |
| Currency | US-style dollars. Figures in fan sources: tens of thousands for a low vestige trade, millions for organs/debts, tens of millions for a multi-affinity Prime Vestige. Banks and accounts are legally untouchable, even for the strong (the economy collapsed repeatedly until this rule existed). | [TVT] | MED |
| The Empire | "The American Empire", founded early post-Rift, ruled by 5-star Empress Kaiya. Darwinian: city lords have near-absolute local power, tempered by pragmatism and imperial adjudicators. New York is "Nova York". | [WIKI] Empress Kaiya, Nova York; [TVT] | HIGH |
| Northern Belt | Former Canada: a lawless, frozen buffer between the Empire and the "Frozen Wastes", full of wild low-tier passages and realm fragments. Easy to enter, hard to return from (the Empire uses it as a dumping ground). Contains Ottawa and "Repentawa", which the protagonist ends up ruling. | [WIKI] Northern Belt; [AETHON] Book 3/4 blurbs | HIGH |
| Factions | Kraven clan (blood-affinity dynasty, mine owners, main early antagonists); Gem family (crime family / loan sharks); Valhalla (vigilante group in the Belt, led by "Thor"); a cult founded by the first-generation villain "Silver Heart" - wiki names a "Republic of Prosperity"; Madame Morleppe (4-star, 200+ yrs old, media figure and patron); "square-eyed" possessing entities from a far realm that grant power via an "ether virus"; mainland clans that send heirs ("young masters") to gain experience. | [TVT]; [WIKI] character pages, Latent Aura | MED |
| Universal translation | Anyone with a talent (archs and some monsters) understands every other ascended being; mortals do not get this. | [TVT] | MED |
| Undeath | Death-affinity archs can make themselves undead: cells only die to violence, so no ageing, hunger, or blood loss - with a nasty failure mode. The protagonist becomes undead partway through. | [TVT] | MED |
| Satellites | Character sheets on the wiki list a "satellite" with its own star rank (protagonist: a 2-star satellite). Not explained on any page I could reach. | [WIKI] infoboxes | LOW (meaning unknown) |
| "Cursed" growth items | Unique items with percentage bonuses and a "growth condition" (e.g. spill blood; beat enemies who outclass you). Can be absorbed into the soul construct. | [WIKI] Freddy/Progression | MED |
| Stats | No conventional STR/DEX/INT stat block appears in any secondary source. The visible "numbers" are: stars, essence capacity %, ability stage + progress %, item/construct % modifiers, and healing-quality grade. Despite the "LitRPG" label, PFDB tags it as Western cultivation / progression fantasy. | [WIKI]; [PFDB] | MED (absence of evidence) |

### 1.2 How the "1% Lifesteal" talent works

| Point | Description | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core rule | Whenever the holder damages a living thing, 1% of that damage comes back as healing. | [TVT]; [AETHON] | HIGH |
| "Enemy" is broad | It counts any living thing - trees, grass, even fruit being eaten - not just combatants. Supposedly even being rude to someone gave a trickle. | [TVT] | MED |
| Healing grade | Healing is graded Minimal / First-Aid / Natural / Supernatural / Supreme, with essence cost rising steeply. Lifesteal's healing is top-grade (TV Tropes says Supreme; the wiki's Book-5 sheet labels it "Dynamic-quality") - it regrows limbs, fixed a congenital heart defect, reverses ageing. | [TVT]; [WIKI] Freddy/Progression | MED-HIGH |
| Too slow for combat | 1% cannot out-heal incoming damage in a fight, so in-world experts rate the talent as weak. Its real value is out-of-combat: train to destruction, then mow a lawn to recover. | [TVT]; [HINTON] | HIGH |
| Design consequences in the fiction | Enables self-destructive tempering techniques, drug/steroid abuse without side effects, all-offence fighting style, spirit abilities paid in blood/bone, even organ-selling for cash. Healing fast is also a liability when hiding. | [TVT] | MED |
| Acquisition | The protagonist first manifested a farming-type vestige with three affinities, and a broker swapped it for the Lifesteal vestige plus cash. It was a known gamble because healing talents often have crippling fine print. | [TVT]; [PFDB] | MED |
| Ambiguity is intentional | An in-story consultant and at least one reviewer both note that "1% of what?" is deliberately unclear at first and must be tested. | [TVT]; [HINTON] | HIGH |

### 1.3 Latent aura (soul-growth currency)

- Growth toward the next star depends on "latent aura", earned by overcoming genuine difficulty: dangerous kills, building high-quality abilities, crafting, mental trials. [WIKI] Latent Aura - MED.
- You can ascend at the minimum or over-fill for a better ascension; shortcuts (elixirs, extracts) lower the requirement but weaken the result. [WIKI] - MED.
- Things that reduce aura gain per the wiki: many affinities, too many abilities (tipping point around 22 at two stars), fighting in groups, using shortcut items, merely following orders, and complacency. [WIKI] Latent Aura limiters - MED, partly fan inference.
- The "balloon and compressor" explanation of why spirit abilities slow growth but improve the eventual ascension is explicitly one Reddit user's analogy reposted on the wiki. LOW (fan theory).

### 1.4 Protagonist build snapshot (start of Book 5, per wiki)

2 stars; affinities water, blood, and unique "Perished Water"; soul construct "Cycle Scythe"; seven tempering techniques (e.g. a blood-storage technique, a blood-to-metal technique, a water-torture technique with escalating names); roughly 15 active abilities split across blood (knuckle weapons, javelins, shields, scales) and water (strikes, throws, water creation); three spirit abilities: a post-battle blood summon bound to the battle site, a bone-spike leviathan skull attack paid in the user's own body, and a two-phase time-skew ability. Source: [WIKI] Freddy/Progression - MED (single fan source, but detailed and consistent with [TVT]).

### 1.5 INFERRED / uncertain

- "Essence" vs "ether" vs "lifespark": fan sources use all three; my reading is ether = ambient medium, essence = the arch's spendable pool, lifespark = the life-force of a creature (normally only touchable at 4 stars). LOW.
- Whether the series uses classes: no source mentions classes. "Brawler" in the blurbs is marketing shorthand for the fighting style, not a system class. LOW-MED.
- The wiki's "Spirit Construct" page is flagged by its own editor as probably wrong; ignore it.
- TV Tropes is accurate only through roughly Book 3; the wiki main page says four books but has volume pages only for 1-3.

Takeaways for a pitch:
1. No licensed LitRPG **video game** of note turned up; every public deal is tabletop or screen. A digital dungeon-crawler would be relatively novel, which cuts both ways (no precedent terms; no competition).

3. Crowdfunding is the proven financing route for the genre.
